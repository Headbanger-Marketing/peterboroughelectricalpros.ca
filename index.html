Before work begins

Plan each phase so it stands complete on its own.

An older home that has seen years of alterations may have wiring of different ages and methods behind the same walls, and nobody may hold a record of who changed what or when. Records can be missing in any home.

Write down what each budget is meant to cover, then sketch what must be finished, labelled and recorded at the end of every phase, so a later contractor can pick up the work without guessing.

Is it sensible to do electrical work in stages as money allows?

Often it can, provided each phase is finished, safe and documented on its own. Ask the contractor to outline every phase first, then confirm that stopping after any one leaves the property in a sound, labelled condition.

What makes quotes for separate phases hard to line up?

Ask each contractor to state what the phase includes, what it leaves for later and which records come with it. Quotes are only comparable when the boundaries of the work match, so request the same written scope from each.

What if nobody knows who altered the wiring in the past?

Missing records are not unusual in a home that has changed hands or been altered. Gather any invoices, ESA paperwork or inspection reports you can find, and ask the contractor to note what could not be verified in the written scope.

Where do I start if money covers only part of the project?

The contractor should help you order phases by safety and by what later work depends on. Ask which items must come first so that no later phase has to undo or reopen finished work.

Does sending the form arrange any phase of the work?

Do not expect the form to arrange any phase. Use the site for planning; it reviews your enquiry, with no say in visits or prices, or in the electrical work. Get any Licensed Electrical Contractor's name and licence separately and check that licence through ESA first. Then settle scope and price with it directly, and the dates too.
